    How far is the metro? Walking access to STM stations in Montréal

    Many students and residents in Montreal rely on the metro every day, but a station that looks close on a map can be a long or difficult walk. This map helps them see which areas have good walking access to an STM metro station and which do not.

    This map is for

    Students and residents in Montreal who want to check how easy it is to walk to the metro.

    What you can do here

    • Read a station: the fill is the number of open grocery stores within a 500 m walk, and the ring is the metro line. Click it for details, including wheelchair access.
    • See who is left out: tick 5-minute walk areas on the map. Blank space is more than a 500 m walk from any station.
    • Wheelchair users: open the 🔍 Search panel and tick the box to keep only the stations you can use.
    • Type a station name in the 🔍 Search panel to see every grocery store within a 500 m walk, then click a store (in the list or on the map) to see its real walking route. Red routes are detours, more than 1.5 times the straight line, often because of a highway or rail line.

    Not in this iteration

    • Long walks: walks are worked out up to 1,600 m (about 20 minutes). A store further than that from every station, such as in the West Island, shows its straight-line distance instead, and says so. Walk times assume 80 m a minute and ignore hills, stairs and waiting at lights.
    • Wheelchair routes: the walk areas and routes use the walking network, which includes stairs, curbs and steep streets. With the wheelchair box ticked they show the area near an accessible station, not a route checked for a wheelchair.
    • Paths OpenStreetMap does not have: walks through malls, parking lots and the underground city are missing, so a store inside a mall (for example at Angrignon) can look out of reach.
    • A stricter grocery list: the city's grocery category also includes kiosks and pharmacies, and the data stops at the Island of Montréal, so stations in Laval and Longueuil may have a closer store than the one shown.
    • One borough focus: I kept the whole metro network to keep the pipeline simple; next I will focus on Côte-des-Neiges–NDG, where many students live and the Décarie expressway creates walking barriers.
    • Transfer stations: stations like Berri-UQAM and Lionel-Groulx appear under one line only, because that is how the STM file stores them.
